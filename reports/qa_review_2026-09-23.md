# Ashcroft: approach review and full QA — 23 September 2026

Three questions were asked: should the system predict BFSP or its own win probability,
what would Benter and Woods do, and is the approach sound end to end. This answers them on
evidence produced today, and lists every defect the QA found, most severe first. Every
finding was checked against the code or a run log before it went in; the few that were
not are marked.

---

## 1. The answer

**Predict both, for different jobs — but only one of them has shown an edge.**

| Question | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Does our win probability add anything to BSP? (Benter's test: an edge at the closing price) | Stage C — ln p_model and ln p_BSP in a conditional logit on the winner, fitted on earlier folds, scored on the next; ΔR² over BSP with a race bootstrap | **No.** R1: ΔR² −0.00009 (90% CI −0.00016 to −0.00001), 10,599 races. Deployed `logit` recipe: −0.00008 (−0.00019 to +0.00003), 10,413 races. The fitted weight on the model is 0.00–0.04, on BSP ≈ 1.0. |
| Does a market-free, outcome-trained model do better? | §10.5: Stage F, 19 Blandford features, 5,018 races | **No.** ΔR² +0.00001 (−0.00014 to +0.00017). |
| Does our BFSP forecast know where the price will close, beyond the 06:00 card price? (the early-price, closing-line-value edge) | Live, 19–22 Sep, 156 races, 1,501 runners: ln q_BSP on ln q_card and ln q_model | **Yes, on four days.** Model weight 0.39 (95% CI 0.30–0.47, race bootstrap). After fitting the card's own shape, where the model disagrees strongly (561 runners) the BSP moves its way 67% of the time — 71% when it says *shorter*, 64% when it says *longer*. Fitted on 19–20 Sep and scored on 21–22 Sep, adding the model cuts the BSP-forecast error (median 0.352 → 0.338, RMSE 0.637 → 0.608). |

So the two framings answer different questions:

* **"Predict our own win probability and compare against the market"** is the right way to
  look for an edge *at* BSP. It has been tried twice and BSP wins both times. The modern
  Betfair SP explains R² ≈ 0.19 of who wins; Benter's 1980s Hong Kong tote public explained
  0.12. The bar is far higher than the one he cleared.
* **"Predict BFSP"** is the right way to look for an edge *in time* — backing early at a
  price longer than the horse will close at. That is the only place the evidence points,
  and it is still unproven where it matters: the 06:00 card price is a bookmaker-style
  price (median book 1.18) that may not be bettable, and the morning exchange price is not
  being recorded (§4, M9–M10).

**The one change of framing that matters:** a model trained on BSP learns the market's
opinion, errors included. It can forecast the price; it cannot find where the price is
wrong. Anything that is meant to find value has to be trained on the *result* and use the
market as an input, never as the target. The current regression is the right tool for
timing and the wrong tool for selection — and at present it is being used for neither
cleanly, because the live pipeline feeds it different features from the ones it learned
on (C1).

## 2. What Benter and Woods would do here

1. **Data first.** They employed people to hand-record what the public could not see. They
   would not tolerate a six-month hole in the results, five more days lost to a scrape that
   believed an empty page, a Betfair price job that has been green for weeks while loading
   nothing, or a live card that reaches the model with a fifth of its features missing.
   Most of this review is that list.
2. **Model the outcome.** A fundamental model on the winner (multinomial/conditional logit
   then; a race-softmax GBM now — `model/stage_f.py` has both), trained without market
   inputs.
3. **Combine with the public, and judge only the combination.** Benter's fundamental model
   was *worse* than the public on its own; the money came from the second stage
   (α ln f + β ln π), accepted only if it beat the public out of sample. That is ΔR² — the
   number in §1 that is currently zero.
4. **Bet where the public is weakest, with Kelly on the real price.** For them: the late
   tote and the exotic pools, where public errors compound. Here: the early exchange
   market, which is thinner and less efficient than BSP (the §1 signal), and possibly place
   and exotic pools priced from fitted ordering (`model/ordering.py`, γ≈0.75, δ≈0.67).
   Kelly net of commission, fractional, and only once the edge is measured — accounting
   for your own effect on a thin morning book.
5. **One pipeline, and verify everything.** The features a bet is placed on must be built
   by the same code, from the same kind of rows, as the features the model was trained on.

2. **Fix the live feature path and the same-day lag (C1, C2), then retrain and re-evaluate.**

## 4. QA findings, most severe first

### Critical

**C1. The live card reaches the model with different features from training.**
Today's predict run (35825688579) logs `CSV download returned no data, trying HTML
scrape...`, so the HTML path is what serves. Its runner rows
(`daily_predictions.py:284-345`) carry the race fields plus stall, horse, age, weight and
jockey, and none of `dist_furlongs`, `race_type`, `surface_type`, `horse_sex`, `stallion`,
`dam_stallion`, `career_runs`, `jockeys_claim`, `max_or_in_race`, `median_or`,
`track_direction`. The CSV path (`:411`) also lacks `MedianOR` and `damstallion`.
A missing category is encoded as −1 (`model/bfsp_features.py:685-692`), whose comment says
LightGBM "treats [it] as missing". With `native_categoricals: false` it does not: −1 is a
number below every split, scored exactly as code 0 (checked on a toy booster). So live,
every race is read as race type `Beginners Chase`, surface `Beach`, sex `Colt` — the first
entries in the stored vocabulary. The review's probe put the HTML path's changed features
at 137 of 501 (18% of gain), 39 of them always NaN, including `or_vs_max` (#11 by gain),
`median_or_num` (#12) and `career_runs_num` (#31). The out-of-sample evaluation is built
from result rows and never sees any of it. Live median error 19–22 Sep was 0.435 against
0.355 out of sample; stale form, a stale model and this mismatch all contribute, in
proportions not yet measured.
*Fix:* one column map shared with `scraper.py`; derive field-level columns (max/median OR,
runners) from the card itself; refuse to serve a feature that is 100% NaN; map unseen
categories to NaN, not −1.

**C2. Earlier results from the same day feed later races in training.**
`model/lagsafe.py:85-100` sorts races by date, time and id and steps back one *race*, so a
jockey's 3.30 features include his 2.30 result. At 06:00 none of today's results exist;
live, the card rows carry invented outcomes instead (`won` is 0 when `placing` is empty;
EPF defaults to 3.0), so a jockey's fifth ride sees four invented losses. The review's
probe: shuffling Ascot 2.30's results changed 60 features of Ascot 3.30 (8.3% of gain) —
the inputs to `trainerWAXrank` (#7), `jockeyWAXrank` (#10), `jockeyCWOrank` (#16) and
more. `tests/test_leakage.py:95` calls earlier same-card races "fair game"; for a 06:00
forecast they are not. *Fix:* lag by day; blank the target day's outcome columns before
building features. Every out-of-sample figure to date includes this.

### High

**H1. Execution would bet real money with broken guards.** `execute.yml` is disabled (since
19 Aug, after 1,251 runs). Every run in the logs that still exist (25 Jun–19 Aug) died at
`BETFAIR_APP_KEY must be set`, so none placed a bet; earlier logs have expired — your
Betfair statement would settle it. If it is re-enabled:
* it is live by default (`DRY_RUN: ${{ inputs.dry_run || 'false' }}`, `dry_run: false`);
* Kelly ignores commission (`src/ultra_betting/rules/staking.py:52-56`: b = price − 1) —
  at the 10% edge floor it stakes ~1.7× the correct amount (p=0.2 at 5.5: 2.22% vs 1.29%);
* the entry rule (live back ≥ 1.10 × forecast) has never been backtested;
* per the operations review (not all re-checked line by line): the stored order status is
  never "MATCHED", so the daily stop-loss never fires and settlement never settles; the
  timing window and in-play exclusion are not enforced; there is no `concurrency:` or
  timeout, and the executions record is written only at the end of a run, so a crash
  after placing bets means the next run places them again.
*Fix:* keep it off; default to dry run; fix the guards before any live use.

**H4. Nothing alarms on stale data or empty output.** Predict runs 31869472645 (15 Aug)

**H5. Leaky figures stood in RESEARCH_FRAMEWORK.md §12 and drove a recommendation.**

### Low

From the three reviews, not each re-checked line by line: dead heats settled at full odds (`bet_analysis.py:61`); Stage F's "fundamental" R² gives
unratable runners the market's probability (`train_stage_f.py:218-224`); the training
summary's "final model" metrics are in-sample; bootstraps cluster by race, not meeting;
`track_draw_bias` is always NaN and would leak if "fixed" (`draw_metrics.py:608-622`);
FSARB's median scale and the evaluation's category vocabulary come from the whole frame;
the nightly BSP sync can only update rows that already exist; certificate login never
runs (`~` not expanded); settlement runs at 21:00 for today only.

**Tested and rejected:** replacing the proportional price normalisation with an exact
logit-shift inverse (as the evaluation review suggested) is *worse* on 106,864 runners —
mean |log error| 0.4639 vs 0.4617, paired race-level difference −0.0021 (95% CI −0.0025
to −0.0017). The serving code stays as it is.

### Checked and clean

No feature reads its own race's result, BSP, SP, comment or time (every result field
shuffled). The market-derived features use only earlier races' prices. The §13 fixes hold.
Walk-forward folds are anchored, split by date and purged 30 days; early stopping never
touches the scored fold. Murphy decomposition, concordance, McFadden R² and ΔR², and the
multi-runner Kelly solution are correct. The served feature code matches the trained
model's hash, and the category vocabulary is stored with the model and reused.
