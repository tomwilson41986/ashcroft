# Greyhound exchange trading: literature review for "take a price, trade out at the off"

racing² - prepared 2 November 2026. Scope: UK greyhound WIN markets on Betfair Exchange; the strategy backs (or lays) a few minutes before the off and closes at BSP or the last pre-off book, so profit is roughly price_taken / BSP - 1 (closing-line value, CLV).

Verification note: every source below was located on the web during this review. Where only an abstract or repository record was read, the finding is labelled "(abstract)". Where a figure comes from a secondary source or could not be checked, it is labelled "unverified". Full texts actually read: Benter (1994), Butler, Butler & Gaine (2025, accepted manuscript), Brown & Yang (2017, JSE, revised manuscript), Lyons (2016), Gulati & Shetty (UWI conference paper), Dzalbs & Kalganova (Brunel), Franck et al. (2010, first pages), and the two Betfair Data Scientists tutorials.

## 1. Executive summary (10 lines)

1. Benter (1994) is the template for our problem and diagnoses our main symptom: a fundamental model's probabilities are biased towards the public's exactly on the runners it would bet, so it must be refitted in a second-stage conditional logit with the market, and judged by its gain in pseudo-R² over the market, not by its stand-alone fit.
2. Our model (log loss 0.451 against 0.431 at T-1 and 0.426 at BSP) is weaker than the market alone. What counts is whether it adds anything once combined with the T-3 book. Benter's tipster example shows a model with a good stand-alone score can add nothing.
3. Late money is informed. In tote markets, money arriving in the last minute or the last five minutes predicts results beyond the final odds (Gramm & McKinney 2009; Hanyu et al., arXiv). This supports treating the T-3 to BSP drift as partly predictable. It also explains why the dogs we pick drift: informed money opposes our disagreements with the market.
4. Betfair's own data scientists find the market sharpens steadily into the off, with BSP the most efficient price. At the scheduled off the best back price predicts BSP better than Betfair's projected SP (mean absolute error 9.2% against 12.1%). In the final book the geometric mid is best (4.0%). A small model with BSP-pool features improves slightly (8.9%). This is practitioner evidence on Australian thoroughbreds.
5. Short-horizon order-book signals are real but modest. Order-flow imbalance (Cont et al. 2014) and queue imbalance (Gould & Bonart 2015) predict the next mid move, most strongly when the tick is large relative to the spread, as on Betfair. In Betfair's tutorial, weight of money and the last 10 seconds of traded-price movement correlate with the next 30 seconds' tick move at |rho| of about 0.26 and 0.32.
6. Greyhound favourite-longshot evidence is mixed and mostly comes from US tote pools. Studies find the classic bias, the reverse bias, or none (Weinbach & Paul 2008; Gulati & Shetty 2007; Terrell 1998). Shin-type corrections beat raw prices in 1,000 greyhound races (Cain, Law & Peel 2002). We should calibrate by price band on our own BSP, not assume a direction.
7. Trap bias: Butler, Butler & Gaine (2025, Review of Behavioral Economics) find that Trap 4 lowers win probability by about 1-2 percentage points in 2018 GB 525-yard races, and that the bookmaker SP does not price this. Position at the first bend matters (correlation 0.33 with finishing position), but the trap barely predicts it.
8. On exchanges, makers earn more than takers, and takers lose more on longshots (Whelan 2025). Resting orders carry adverse-selection risk (Glosten & Milgrom 1985). However, Brown & Yang found no adverse-selection component in pre-race horse prices. Passive entry at T-5 to T-3, measured by edge conditional on a fill, is the main execution test.
9. Thin books favour non-exchange prices. Bookmakers beat the exchange when exchange liquidity is low (Flepp, Nüesch & Franck 2017), while the exchange leads when liquid (Franck et al. 2010; Croxson & Reade 2011). Early greyhound books are hollow, so bookmaker and early-price information is a candidate feature.
10. Staking: Benter warns that overestimating the edge by a factor of two makes full Kelly lose money, and he used 1/2 to 1/3 Kelly. Baker & McHale (2013) formalise shrinkage. For a trade-out, size should be Kelly on the CLV distribution, capped by the depth at T-3 (median GBP380 matched).

## 3. Topic sections

### 3.1 Greyhound racing: efficiency, bias, prediction, traps, sectionals

**Market efficiency and favourite-longshot bias.** Almost all greyhound-specific efficiency work uses US tote pools, not UK bookmaker or exchange prices. The results conflict.
- Weinbach & Paul (2008): longshots overbet at Dairyland over more than 45,000 races. The bias weakens once off-track money arrives, and it appears only in maiden races.
- Gulati & Shetty (2007, JEF): a reverse bias (favourites overbet) at three tracks. Their conference paper on 4,723 races at one track finds no classic bias.
- Terrell (1998): favourites and trap 7 overbet, with signs of learning over time.

Ottaviani & Sørensen (2010) offer a single explanation: the sign depends on informed money relative to noise. On that view, UK BAGS afternoon cards and evening open races could differ. Cain, Law & Peel (2002), on 1,000 greyhound races, show that probabilities derived from the whole book (Shin) beat transformations of single prices.

No peer-reviewed study of GB greyhound Betfair prices or BSP was found ("no data found"). Our own BSP calibration is therefore the primary evidence.

**Prediction models.**
- Chen et al. (1994): a neural network on expert-chosen variables beat track experts (details via secondary source, unverified).
- Johansson & Sönströd (2003): known only from Lyons' citation.
- Schumaker & Johnson (2008, SVR): a 45% strike rate still lost on win bets.
- Lyons (2016): the machine-selected features were sectional time, break, 2nd-bend position, average times, grade, age and weight. The models did not beat the track expert's 23.7% strike rate.
- Betfair's Australian greyhound tutorial: normalised run time and split against track and distance winners, box win rates, and rolling windows.

None of these benchmark against BSP or measure CLV, and none uses a Benter-style second stage. There is no published greyhound Benter-style application ("no data found"); the method transfers from horse racing (sections 3.3 and 4).

**Trap and draw bias.** Butler, Butler & Gaine (2025) is the only rigorous GB study found. In 2018 GB 525-yard races, Trap 4 cut win probability by about 1-2 percentage points even after controlling for SP. Trap 1 was advantaged. Gaine (2022) found Trap 3 also disadvantaged, and that dropping either middle trap advantages the other: a crowding effect.

Two caveats limit the "not priced" claim:
- SP enters the ordered logit linearly, so curvature in the price-outcome relationship could appear as a trap effect.
- It is bookmaker SP from a single season, not BSP.

A practitioner report says Tralee's "coffin" trap 4 disadvantage vanished after resurfacing ([Kerryman via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/ireland/the-kerryman-north-kerry/20120118/291572446435789), practitioner). Trap effects are track- and surface-specific and may change over time.

**Sectionals and race shape.** Butler et al. find bend-1 position is strongly linked to the result (correlation 0.334), and bend 4 matters most. The trap itself barely predicts bend-1 position (0.01-0.05). The leverage is therefore in predicting who leads at the first bend from early-pace history (sectional or split times, break), and in conflicts of early pace between adjacent traps. Lyons' feature ranking agrees (sectional average, break average, 2nd-bend position).

### 3.2 Exchange microstructure and price dynamics before the off

**Is BSP efficient?** Exchanges show smaller biases than bookmakers (Smith, Paton & Vaughan Williams 2006), and are more accurate (Franck et al. 2010). Betfair's tutorial shows that a calibration plot cannot measure efficiency; log loss can. On that measure the market sharpens steadily to BSP (0.27626 at T-120 s, 0.27582 at T-0, 0.27568 at BSP; Australian thoroughbreds). Our greyhound numbers (BSP 0.426 against T-1 0.431) show the same ordering with a larger gap, as expected for thinner books.

Implication: BSP is the right target and the right exit. Our edge must come from forecasting the move from the price we take to BSP, not from beating BSP on the result.

**Late money and informed trading.**
- Tote evidence: late money is informed (Gramm & McKinney 2009), and late odds declines predict returns beyond the final odds (Hanyu et al., arXiv; secondary summary).
- Exchange evidence:
  - Brown & Yang (2017, RoF): volume carries information mainly in-running.
  - Brown & Yang (2017, JSE): no adverse-selection component in pre-race horse prices.
  - Marginson (2010): lay-side known-loser activity may be common.
  - Law & Peel (2002) is directly on plungers and herding, but its findings were not accessed.

In practice: the dogs our model picks drift, and late money is the most informed. Our disagreements with the market are therefore partly the market knowing something we do not (Benter's Table 4 shows the same thing in tote data).

**Liquidity timing.** Betfair's tutorial finds volume doubling from T-10 to T-4, again by T-1, and nearly again in the final minute (Victorian thoroughbreds). In US tote pools, about 40% of money comes in the last minute (Gramm & McKinney). Our greyhound profile is more extreme (median GBP34 at T-5, GBP380 at T-3, GBP1,993 at T-1). When the exchange is thin, bookmakers offer better prices (Flepp et al. 2017), which fits our hollow early books and the thin Betfair BASIC first prints.

**Predictability of the drift.** Betfair's tutorial shows that backing large movers after the move, at the off, gave no edge. Short-horizon (30 s) moves correlate with weight of money and recent traded-price movement. No peer-reviewed study of greyhound pre-off drift predictability was found ("no data found"). The two Betfair machine-learning papers found (Gonçalves et al. 2025; Dzalbs & Kalganova) are horse racing, and their results are either unread or likely overfitted.

**Maker against taker.** Whelan (2025) predicts, and finds in Betfair soccer, that makers earn more than takers, and that takers' losses grow on longshots. Brown & Yang found trade-weighted back and lay prices both well calibrated in horse racing. That suggests taking costs little in liquid books, but it was not tested on greyhounds.

### 3.3 Combining a fundamental model with market prices, and forecasting the close

**Benter's two-stage model** (read in full). Fit a fundamental model, take its out-of-sample probabilities f, and fit c_i ∝ exp(α log f_i + β log π_i) by conditional maximum likelihood, where π is the public's probability. The heuristic profitability measure is ΔR² = R²(combined) - R²(public). His example: a 9-factor model with R² 0.1016 adds 0.0090 over the public's 0.1237. Tipsters with a near-identical stand-alone R² (0.1014) add 0.0002.

Benter also documents that the fundamental model's errors lean towards the public's view on exactly the horses it would bet (Tables 3-4). That is our "overrates the dogs it selects". He warns that profitability "will suffer greatly" if the final odds differ from those used, and that small pools have volatile pre-post odds. That is our T-3 problem.

**Bolton & Chapman (1986)** introduced the multinomial and rank-ordered logit. **Lessmann, Sung & Johnson** (2007, 2010, 2012) extend it:
- ranking-aware tuning (NDCG);
- random forests adapted to within-race competition;
- stacking diverse base models through a conditional logit, where diversity matters as much as strength.

Johnson & Sung (2007) compare one-step and two-step conditional logits directly, but the paper could not be accessed.

**Transfer to forecasting the close rather than the result.** No paper was found that fits a Benter second stage to the closing price rather than the result ("no data found"). The nearest is Betfair's tutorial, which predicts BSP from the book at the off: best back, mid, near and far prices, and a pool-fill WAP. The natural adaptation for us has two parts:
- Second stage for the BSP book: fit the conditional logit with the T-3 book's probabilities, our form model's out-of-sample log-probability, and book-state covariates as inputs. Use the BSP-implied probabilities (normalised) as soft targets with cross-entropy. This is a "closing-price model" in Benter's form.
- Result check: also fit the same model on results, to check that any closing edge is consistent with result edge.

Ottaviani & Sørensen and Hanyu et al. suggest the path to the close carries information, so the book-state covariates should include path variables. Benter's ΔR² over the T-3 book, with BSP as the target, becomes the headline metric for every feature block.

### 3.4 Limit-order-book short-horizon prediction for a thin exchange

What transfers to a few minutes of 1-3 second snapshots:
- **Order-flow imbalance (Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov 2014):** the change in best-back and best-lay sizes and prices between snapshots, summed over a window. Impact per unit of OFI is inversely proportional to depth, so in greyhound books at T-3 our own GBP15-200 orders will move prices.
- **Queue imbalance (Gould & Bonart 2015):** a logistic regression of the next mid-move on top-level imbalance. It works best for large-tick instruments. The Betfair ladder (e.g. 0.02 increments around 2-3, 0.1 around 5-6, 0.2 around 6-10, 0.5 around 10-20) is large-tick relative to typical greyhound spreads once the book fills.
- **Micro-price (Stoikov 2018):** an imbalance- and spread-adjusted fair value. A replication found no consistent gain (secondary), so treat it as a candidate only.
- **Practitioner Betfair features:** weight of money, top-of-book support against the top-3 VWAP, and the last 10 s of traded-price movement.

What does not transfer well: DeepLOB-style deep networks (Zhang et al. 2019) and the convolutional-attention models (Gonçalves et al. 2025) need dense event streams. A greyhound market with GBP34 matched at T-5 has too few events per race. LightGBM on hand-built features, pooled across races with time-to-off as a feature, is the realistic route; Betfair's tutorial recommends the same pooling.

### 3.5 Staking and execution for trade-out strategies

**Fractional Kelly under estimation error.** Benter: overestimating the edge by a factor of two makes Kelly growth negative, and "overestimating the advantage by a factor of two is easily done in practice". He used 1/2 to 1/3 Kelly. Baker & McHale (2013) derive the shrinkage for parameter uncertainty. Uhrín et al. (2021) find adaptive fractional Kelly robust in practice.

For a trade-out, the bet's outcome is approximately the CLV (price_taken / BSP - 1) rather than win or lose. Its variance is much smaller, but its mean is small and estimated with error. Size should therefore follow the predicted CLV distribution, shrunk for uncertainty, and be capped by the depth available (Cont et al.: impact scales inversely with depth).

**Passive against aggressive.** Makers earn more than takers on Betfair (Whelan 2025). Glosten & Milgrom (1985) imply that resting orders are filled disproportionately when informed money arrives, which in greyhounds is the last 1-3 minutes. Brown & Yang found no adverse-selection premium in liquid pre-race horse markets. The right test is empirical, on our live_orders: CLV conditional on a fill for passive orders, against CLV for aggressive fills, by time-to-off and price band.

**Exit at BSP against the last book.** Betfair's tutorial shows the last-book geometric mid is within about 4% of BSP on average (thoroughbreds). The projected near price is cached for 60 s and is a poor guide. The BSP lay leg settles at the reconciled price, so the exit is guaranteed but unknown in advance. No study found compares the costs of exiting at BSP against exiting in the last book ("no data found").

## 4. Concrete things to test

Each item names the source(s) that motivate it and the data it needs from our store: the T-3/T-1 recorded books (best back and lay prices and sizes, matched money), GBGB form and sectionals, BSP, and live_orders.

1. **Benter second stage on the T-3 book (main change).** Fit a conditional logit c ∝ exp(α log f_model + β log π_T-3) using out-of-sample (walk-forward) model probabilities. Report ΔR² = R²(combined) - R²(T-3 book), with both the result and the BSP book as targets. If ΔR² against BSP is near zero, the form model adds nothing to the trade, whatever its stand-alone log loss. *Sources:* Benter 1994; Bolton & Chapman 1986. *Data:* GBGB model OOS probabilities, T-3 book, BSP, results.
2. **Re-measure "the dogs it selects drift" after the second stage.** Recompute the 7-12% drift on the runners selected by the combined model. Benter's Table 4 bias should shrink. If drift persists, it is late informed money, not model bias. *Sources:* Benter 1994; Gramm & McKinney 2009; Hanyu et al. *Data:* live_orders, BSP.
3. **Trade the disagreement both ways.** Test whether (model - market) disagreement at T-3 predicts the sign of log(BSP / price at T-3). If dogs the model favours drift systematically, a lay at T-3 closed at BSP may carry positive CLV, after spread and commission. Treat this as a hypothesis to test, not a finding. *Sources:* Benter 1994 Table 4; Marginson 2010. *Data:* T-3 book, BSP.
4. **Whole-book probabilities, not 1/price.** Convert the T-3 back/lay book to probabilities by Shin, power and logit transforms. Pick by out-of-sample log loss against results and against BSP, separately for back and lay sides and mid. *Sources:* Cain, Law & Peel 2002; Snowberg & Wolfers 2010. *Data:* T-3 book, BSP, results.
5. **BSP calibration by price band, grade and meeting type.** Measure the favourite-longshot pattern on GB greyhound BSP: BAGS against evening cards, open against graded against puppy. Check whether it differs at T-3 against BSP, since a difference is CLV. *Sources:* Weinbach & Paul 2008; Gulati & Shetty 2007; Ottaviani & Sørensen 2010. *Data:* BSP, T-3 book, GBGB grade, results.
6. **Trap effects residual to the market.** Add trap dummies (by track, distance and surface era) to the second stage. Test whether Trap 4 (and 3) are negative and Trap 1 positive after conditioning on log π_T-3, and separately after log π_BSP. Priced at BSP but not at T-3 means CLV; priced at neither means result edge only. Use log-probability controls, not linear odds. *Sources:* Butler, Butler & Gaine 2025; Gaine 2022; Terrell 1998. *Data:* GBGB trap, BSP, T-3 book.
7. **First-bend model.** Predict each dog's bend-1 position from early sectionals or splits, break history and neighbours' early pace. Feed the predicted bend-1 rank, and crowding terms (adjacent fast-breakers), into the win model. *Sources:* Butler et al. 2025 (bend 1 correlates 0.334 with finish; trap barely predicts bend 1); Lyons 2016 (sectional, break and bend-2 features). *Data:* GBGB sectionals and bend positions.
8. **Track- and distance-normalised times.** Check that run time and split are expressed relative to the track and distance median of winners, with rolling 28/91/365-day windows and a track-speed index. *Sources:* Betfair Greyhound Modelling tutorial; Lyons 2016. *Data:* GBGB results.
9. **Closing-price baselines.** Before any new feature, benchmark mean absolute percentage error and log loss against BSP for: best back, best lay, ladder mid, geometric mid and micro-price at T-3, T-1 and the last book. Betfair's thoroughbred figures are 9.2% (best back at the off) and 4.0% (geometric mid in the last book). The closing-price model must beat the best of these in greyhounds. *Sources:* Betfair BSP tutorial; Stoikov 2018. *Data:* recorded books, BSP.
10. **Order-book features in the closing-price model.**
    - Top-level queue imbalance: (back size - lay size) / (back size + lay size).
    - OFI over the last 10/30/60 s, divided by depth.
    - Top-of-book support against the top-3 VWAP.
    - Change in matched volume.
    - Traded-price (WAP) change over the last 10 s.
    - Spread in ticks.
    - Time to off.

    Pool rows across snapshots from T-5 to T-1 with time-to-off as a feature. *Sources:* Cont et al. 2014; Gould & Bonart 2015; Betfair market-movement tutorial (|rho| about 0.32 for WAP move, 0.26 for weight of money). *Data:* 1-3 s snapshots, matched money.
11. **Path features.** Add the price change from T-5 to T-3, its acceleration, and the runner's share of matched money gained since T-5. Test whether they predict log(BSP / price now) beyond the current book. *Sources:* Hanyu et al. (arXiv); Gramm & McKinney 2009. *Data:* recorded books.
12. **BSP-pool features where recorded.** If SP_AVAILABLE was recorded (from 8 Oct), add near and far prices with their timestamps and the back-pool and lay-liability WAP-to-fill features. Never use the near price without its update time: it is cached for 60 s. *Source:* Betfair BSP tutorial. *Data:* recorded SP projections.
13. **Bookmaker or early prices as the "price now" before T-5.** Where a bookmaker price or forecast SP is available, test it against the hollow exchange book as an input to the closing-price model. *Sources:* Flepp, Nüesch & Franck 2017; Franck et al. 2010; Croxson & Reade 2011. *Data:* any bookmaker prices held (availability unverified).
14. **Maker against taker execution.** A/B test in paper or live trading: (a) take the best price at T-3, against (b) post at or one tick inside the best price from T-5 and cancel at T-1. Compare CLV conditional on a fill, fill rates, and the fill-time drift ("did the price move against us right after the fill?"). Split by price band, since takers lose more on longshots. *Sources:* Whelan 2025; Glosten & Milgrom 1985; Brown & Yang 2017 (JSE). *Data:* live_orders, recorded books, BSP.
15. **Own-impact and size limits.** Estimate price impact per GBP against depth at T-3 and T-1 from our own fills. Cap the stake at the size whose expected impact stays well below the expected CLV. *Source:* Cont et al. 2014. *Data:* live_orders, book snapshots.
16. **Shrunk Kelly on CLV.** Size each trade on the bootstrap distribution of predicted CLV per runner (Baker & McHale shrinkage), starting at no more than 1/3 Kelly with adaptive scaling on drawdown. *Sources:* Benter 1994; Baker & McHale 2013; Uhrín et al. 2021. *Data:* walk-forward CLV predictions and outcomes.
17. **Exit choice.** Compare closing at BSP with closing in the last pre-off book (at the geometric mid or by taking), net of spread and commission, by price band. *Source:* Betfair BSP tutorial (last-book mid within about 4% of BSP; thoroughbreds). *Data:* recorded last books, BSP, live_orders.
18. **Guard against selection bias in evaluation.** Use walk-forward tests over several months and many configurations, with a reality-check correction. Do not select one strategy from many on one month of data. *Source:* the Dzalbs & Kalganova result (best of 36 agents, 83% ROI over one month) as a counter-example. *Data:* all of the above.
